Services

Care by life stage

Every consultation runs a minimum of twenty minutes. You will always leave with a written summary of what was said and what happens next.

Antenatal care package

A full pregnancy plan — visit schedule, scans, blood work, vaccinations, nutrition and a birth plan discussion from week 32.

High-risk pregnancy

Dedicated pathways for pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, thyroid disease, twins and previous loss. Closer monitoring, clearer escalation — with NICU standby for every delivery.

Painless (epidural) labour

Anaesthetist-supported labour analgesia, with an honest conversation about what it does and does not change.

Caesarean section

Planned and emergency, with skin-to-skin in theatre wherever it is safe, and enhanced recovery so you're mobile the same day.

VBAC assessment

A structured evaluation of whether a vaginal birth after caesarean is reasonable for you — with numbers, not opinions.

Postnatal & lactation

Six-week recovery review, wound care, mental-health check, contraception counselling and hands-on feeding support.

Diagnostic & operative laparoscopy

Keyhole surgery for cysts, adhesions, ectopic pregnancy and endometriosis. Smaller scars, shorter stay, faster return to work.

Hysteroscopy

Camera assessment and treatment inside the uterus — polyps, septum, adhesions, and unexplained bleeding.

Fibroids & myomectomy

Fertility-sparing removal wherever possible. A hysterectomy is a last option here, not a first suggestion.

Heavy or irregular bleeding

Proper work-up before treatment — anaemia correction, hormonal options, and surgery only when it is genuinely indicated.

Screening & prevention

Pap smear, HPV testing and vaccination, breast examination, and a clear recall schedule so nothing gets forgotten.

Infections & pelvic pain

Discreet, thorough assessment of discharge, recurrent UTI, and chronic pelvic pain that has been dismissed elsewhere.

PCOS management

Long-term care, not a one-off prescription — metabolic screening, weight and insulin support, cycle regulation and fertility planning.

Infertility evaluation

Both partners assessed together. A written plan within two visits, with costs stated up front.

Ovulation induction & IUI

Monitored cycles with follicular tracking, and a firm limit on how many attempts are reasonable before escalating.

IVF coordination

Work-up, planning and follow-up here; the laboratory cycle at a partnered centre, with your care staying in one pair of hands.

Recurrent pregnancy loss

Full work-up for repeated miscarriage, targeted treatment, and intensive support through the next pregnancy.

Fertility preservation

Counselling on egg freezing and options before cancer treatment or planned delay.

Adolescent gynaecology

Painful or absent periods, acne and hair changes, vaccination, and a first consultation that doesn't frighten anybody.

Contraception counselling

Every option explained plainly — pills, IUDs, implants, permanent methods — with side effects stated honestly.

Perimenopause

For the decade nobody warns you about: cycle chaos, sleep, mood, brain fog, and what is actually treatable.

Menopause & HRT

Evidence-based hormone therapy where appropriate, bone and heart risk assessment, and non-hormonal alternatives.

Bone & pelvic health

Osteoporosis screening, pelvic floor assessment, prolapse and incontinence care.

Sexual health

A private, non-judgemental space for pain, dryness, libido and relationship concerns at any age.

This practice was built around that conversation. Consultations are not stacked ten to the hour. You get an explanation in the language you think in, a written summary you can show your family, and a direct number for when something worries you at the weekend.

Free tools

Work it out for yourself

Everything below runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent to us, nothing is saved, and you don't need to give a name or number.

Estimated due date

Based on the first day of your last menstrual period (Naegele's rule).

Please choose a date within the last 10 months.

What this number really means

Only about one baby in twenty arrives on the estimated date. Anything from 37 to 42 weeks is considered term. Treat this as the middle of a window, not a deadline — and if an early scan gave you a different date, trust the scan.

When to call us instead of calculating

Bleeding, severe or one-sided pain, a fever, reduced fetal movement after 28 weeks, a sudden gush of fluid, or a headache with blurred vision. Any of these, call — don't wait for the next appointment.

Fertile window

Most useful if your cycles are fairly regular. If they aren't, that itself is worth a consultation.

Please choose a valid recent date.

How to use this

The fertile window is roughly the five days before ovulation plus the day itself, because sperm survive far longer than the egg does. Intercourse every second day across that window is as effective as daily, and considerably less stressful.

Come and see us if

You've been trying for twelve months under 35, or six months over 35 · your cycles are shorter than 21 or longer than 35 days · periods have stopped · there's significant pain · or there's a known issue such as PCOS, endometriosis or previous pelvic surgery.

PCOS self-check

Eight questions. This is a conversation-starter, not a test, and it cannot diagnose anything.

What a real diagnosis involves

PCOS is diagnosed when at least two of three things are present: irregular or absent ovulation, clinical or biochemical signs of excess androgens, and polycystic ovaries on ultrasound — with other causes excluded first. That means a history, an examination, blood tests and a scan. No questionnaire, and no single blood test, can do it.

Why it's worth checking

PCOS is manageable, and managing it early protects fertility, metabolic health and mood. Most of the treatment is not medication.

Pre-pregnancy BMI & healthy weight gain

Uses your weight before pregnancy, or your current weight if you're not pregnant.

Enter a height between 120 and 215 cm.
Enter a weight between 28 and 220 kg.

A note on the number

BMI is a blunt instrument. It ignores muscle, bone structure and where weight sits on your body, and South Asian populations carry metabolic risk at lower BMI values than the standard cut-offs suggest. We use it as a starting point for a conversation about food, movement and blood sugar — never as a verdict on anybody.

In pregnancy

Recommended gain ranges come from the Institute of Medicine guidelines and are adjusted individually. Steady gain matters more than the total.
